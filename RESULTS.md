# auto-scout results

Matches processed: 131 (262 alliance-matches): every 2026 match of team 5026 with a downloadable video (76; Silicon Valley, Glendale, NorCal State Champs, Johnson division, Chezy Champs) plus 55 other Chezy Champs qualification matches that FRC 5026 scouted (human per-team reference). Generated by `scripts/evaluate.py`.

**Summary.** Alliance FUEL totals read from the broadcast's live counter are within a few percent of TBA's official hub counts in every match (the acceptance bar was 15 %). Per-team numbers split that exact total by which robots were seen launching fuel while in their alliance zone, with weights from a small credit model fitted once to 5026's scouting. Each match's split uses only that match's video: no OPR or other team history moves balls between teams. Nothing uses an event's later matches either: the DINO robot recognizer only learns from matches already played, as it would live. A robot or launch no camera can identify goes, by elimination, to the one robot of its alliance not seen anywhere else. Balls are tracked at 60 frames a second where the webcast really has 60 pictures a second (every Chezy match, some Championship ones); most FIRST webcasts show each 30 fps picture twice, and those stay at 30. Against the scouting the per-team numbers are off by ~40 fuel per team-match held out (the older hand-tuned rule on the same evidence: ~46); arcs credited to a teammate standing next to the shooter, and shooters no camera sees, are the main error sources. A pure vision hub counter (no overlay) was built and evaluated but is ~30 % off, so it is only a fallback.

## 3-D arc check for which robot launched a ball (2026-10-18, not adopted)

**Question.** A launch arc is tied to the robot box it rises from. When two boxes overlap in one view, the 2-D
box rule (`dist + TOP_TIEBREAK * top + LINK_X_TIE * xc`) is close to a coin flip for teammates. In the end
cameras a near robot's box also hides a far shooter. Robots have fused field positions and the hub is a known
3-D target. Does a single-view ballistic fit pick the shooter better?

**Answer.** It picks the right robot more often, but per-team fuel does not move.

* **Links.** On the failure finder's 1,084 scout-judged launches, adding the arc term to today's score takes
  pick accuracy from **71.4 % to 74.4 %** (+3.0 pp, 95 % CI +1.0 .. +4.9). Same-alliance picks go from
  61.3 % to 65.3 %.
  * A truth set rebuilt the same way from today's labels gives the same answer: 73.5 → 76.5 % (+3.0,
    +1.1 .. +5.2).
  * Scored as what the pipeline would credit, the gain holds only if the launch gate is decided on today's 2-D
    winner and the arc term just picks the robot: +3.0 and +2.8 pp. As a plain score term it is −1.0 / −1.8 pp.
    The section on the launch gate explains why.
  * The 62.5 % quoted for the current rule predates LINK_X_TIE and the zone rule. Today's rule gets 71.4 % on
    the same cases, so that is the bar.
* **Fuel vs 5026's scouting** (54 Chezy quals, paired by match): video-only fuel MAE −0.04 (CI −0.90 .. +0.80).
  25 matches are better and 26 worse. The links change, but the per-team totals don't.
* **The one-parameter fit as specified does not work.** With the launch fixed 0.55 m above the robot and the
  landing fixed at the hub centre, the true shooter's arc fits no better than a teammate's (+0.2 pp, CI
  −2.6 .. +2.7). The gain needs the launch and landing points free inside priors (below).
* **Recommendation: don't adopt into the fuel pipeline.** It adds about 2.5 s of CPU per match and a 7-parameter
  fitter with tuned constants, for no fuel gain. Port it only if per-launch links matter on their own (the
  inspector, audits), as the zone rule was. The port spec is at the end.

* **With split tracks** (`SPLIT_TRACKS` + `GATE_TRACE`, follow-up below), the arc term is just as flat:
  video-only MAE +0.08 (CI −0.49 .. +0.65) against split + traced gate. Letting it credit robots that have no
  box in the view (254 behind 3847) doesn't reach significance either.

**In plain words.** When two robots overlap on screen, the 3-D check asks which one the ball could have
flown from to reach its hub. It picks the right robot about 3 more times in 100. Per-team fuel ends up the
same, probably because a swap one way between teammates is often matched by a swap the other way (cycle 24
saw the same with every overlap rule).

### Method (`autoscout/arcs.py`, tools in `data/exp_arcs/tools/`)

* **`arcs.arc_fit(track_pts, cam, cand_xy, hub_xyz, launch_z=0.55, max_pts=None, prior=None)`**
  * One view, one camera pose at the launch (`CameraTrack.at(t0)`), view-local px.
  * Drag-free ball from L = (x, y, launch_z) to H = (hub x, hub y, 1.829 m) in flight time T. The velocity is
    (H − L) / T + (0, 0, g T / 2).
  * Free parameters are T in [0.55, 2.6] s and the lead, the launch-to-first-sighting time, in [0, 0.5] s.
  * Fit: a 28 × 16 grid over (T, lead), then `least_squares` with soft-L1 (6 px). The result is the mean
    point cost.
* **`prior=arcs.SOFT_PRIOR`** frees the launch point (dx, dy, dz) and the landing point in the opening (hx, hy).
  * Priors: σ_xy 0.5 m and σ_z 0.25 m around the robot, σ_hub 0.4 m around the hub centre. Image residuals
    are in units of σ_px 4 px.
  * The MAP cost is soft-L1 summed over points plus the prior terms. The one-parameter fit is the start.
  * Why it is needed: with the launch fixed, the true shooter's arc misses by a median 25 px. The first point
    alone is off by 33 px, and the lead sits at its 0 bound in 39 % of fits. The tracks themselves are smooth
    (2.4 px about their own parabola). Robot position error, shooter offset and height swamp the depth signal
    until they are allowed to move.
  * Synthetic check (`tools/synth_check.py`, real Chezy blue_end camera, exact positions): the true launcher
    fits at 2-3 px RMS and a decoy on the same line of sight at 6-55 px, even for 0.13 s arcs.
* **Geometry.** Chezy's end cameras sit at the field corners only about 0.65 m up. A near robot's box top and a
  far one's line up at the same image height, which defeats the 2-D top rule. Gravity curves an arc by
  f · g / depth px/s² (about 2,500 px/s² at 3 m in the end views), and that carries depth.
* **Candidates.**
  * The boxes within the link distance (dist ≤ 0.6) after the zone rule.
  * Position: the box's segment's fused (Kalman) position at the sighting nearest t0, else the box's own
    back-projection.
  * Target: the box's alliance hub.
* **Likelihood ratio.** score += λ · (cost_c − min cost), λ = 0.3. Every candidate is fitted; nothing is
  reconstructed from a single view.
* **Zero-context rule.** Each fit uses only that launch's arc, camera and robot positions. The constants (the
  priors, λ = 0.3) are global, chosen by leave-one-match-out on Chezy, like LINK_X_TIE.
* **Passes.**
  1. `arc_log.py`: `arc_hook.build()` compiles `pipeline.analyse` with one hook call inserted before the zone
     rule, as `pipeline.analyse_arc` in memory. The Logger copy is identical to `pipeline.analyse` (checked on
     cc_qm24). It logs every launch with two or more close candidates (7,770 in 65 matches, 16,690 boxes) to
     `log/<match>.pkl`.
  2. `eval_truth.py`: truth-set scoring from the logs, with fit caches `fits_c24.pkl` / `fits_fresh.pkl`.
  3. `ab_arcs.py`: in-memory A/B, one scan at a time, read with `fh.read()` and closed at once.
     `arc_hook.install_gate_wrapper()` provides the gate on the 2-D winner.
* **Cost.** About 23 ms per candidate fit, 50 ms per contested launch: 2.5 s per match for the gate on the 2-D
  winner (it only fits launches that pass the gate, about 49 per match), 4.6 s for the plain term.

### If it is ported anyway (links only)

In `pipeline.analyse` step (1), after the `cands` loop and the zone rule:

1. Find the winner `best` as today.
2. Only if `best` passes `_box_shot_ok` and two or more pool candidates have `dist <= 0.6`:
   1. For each such candidate, compute `xy` = its segment's fused point nearest t0 (else `d.xy`) and
      `hub = (*hub_center(d.alliance), HUB_OPENING_HEIGHT)`.
   2. Fit: `fits = arcs.arc_fit(tr.pts, cam, xys, hubs, prior=arcs.SOFT_PRIOR)`.
   3. Add `0.3 * (fit["cost"] - min cost)` to each score. A missing fit gets the worst cost.
   4. Link to the new argmin without re-running the gate on it.
3. The reference implementation is `data/exp_arcs/tools/arc_hook.py` `ArcTerm(W=0.3, SIGMA2=1, prior="soft",
   gate_base=True)`. `arcs.py` is sha a8deafa1a224.

## Learned per-team credit split, fitted to 5026's human scouting

**Question.** `attribution.split_increments` shares each scoreboard jump among the alliance's three robots
with the hand-tuned weight `zone * (s + PRIOR*vis + HIDDEN*(1-vis))`. Can a small model fitted to FRC 5026's
scouting of the Chezy Champs quals do better, under honest cross-validation, than that rule and than a
0.7 video + 0.3 fuel-OPR blend of the per-team shares? Does it still hold up at the other events?

**Answer.** Yes, on two of the four scouting metrics. It is the pipeline default since finalize_v4
(`models/attr_model.json`); `result.json` keeps the hand-tuned rule's split as `fuel_video`.

* The learned model cuts the share error from 0.150 (blend) to 0.134 and the team-match fuel error from 49.1
  to 44.5. Both differences have 95 % bootstrap intervals that exclude zero.
* Correlation (0.824 vs 0.817) and top-scorer agreement (65 % vs 66 %) are tied with the blend.
* On the other four events (no scouting), it matches the blend's share correlation with an independent half
  of fuel OPR (0.696 vs 0.696; the current rule gets 0.635) and its top-scorer agreement (60 % vs 60 %).
  Its fuel error against that OPR half is larger than the blend's (39.2 vs 34.9; current rule 40.6). That
  reference has no per-match variation, so it rewards shares squeezed toward OPR, which the scouting shows
  is wrong: the blend's shares have slope 0.50 against scouting, the model's 0.65, ideal 1.
* Nearly all of the gain comes from the OPR prior applied at the match level, together with re-tuning the
  rule's constants. None of the extra video features helped: splitting launch evidence by link kind
  (box / proj / proj_hidden) or by camera view, coverage-based pseudo-shots, or a recall-corrected
  (Gamma-Poisson) rate. A robot's distance to its hub helps a little.

### Data (`tools/build_ds.py`)

* **Source.** After finalize_v3 (`logs/finalize_v3.log` "done in 5437s"), `output/*/result.json` and
  `dets.json` were copied to `final_snapshot/`. `final_snapshot.md5` holds checksums, verified against
  `output/` after finalize. Nothing in `output/` was written.
* **TeamSignals.** These are rebuilt from `result.json` (shots, team paths, overlay counter) exactly as
  `pipeline.analyse` builds them. Re-running the current rule on them reproduces the stored per-team fuel
  to within 3 balls for 256 of the 260 overlay alliances. The other 4 are off by ≤ 4 balls (≤ 2 % of
  share).
* **Rows.** Each scoreboard jump is one row. Each of the three robots has these features:
  * kernel-weighted launch evidence `s`, also split by link kind and by view (own end / other end / main);
  * kernel-weighted launch counts toward / away from the hub;
  * visibility: from the track, per view from `dets.json`, and exclusive coverage (own end / other view
    only / no box);
  * zone fraction;
  * mean distance to its own hub;
  * launches in a ±10 s window and over the whole match.
* **Per alliance.** Fuel-OPR shares (event quals), and each team's typical video share in its other matches
  at the event.
* **Targets.**
  * Match level: scouted shots / alliance scouted shots, averaged over each robot's scouts. Fitting and
    scoring use this target.
  * Per jump: kernel-weighted scouted shot times (`time_valid` rows, tap − 0.8 s, timer = auto start +
    t). Used only as an optional auxiliary loss.
* **Size.** 104 fully scouted alliances in 54 cc quals, 88 of them with usable timestamps for all three
  robots.
* **Noise floor.** On the 90 alliances with two scouts on every robot, the two scouts' shares differ by
  TV 0.128 (correlation 0.843).

### Model (`tools/fit_cv.py`)

For each jump the weights are normalised over the alliance's three robots, so the alliance total stays
exact:

    w_r = (zone_r + 0.001)^gz * (s_r + prior*vis_r + hidden*(1 - vis_r))^b * exp(g_dist * (dist_r - 3.5 m) / 2 m)

* `dist_r` is the robot's mean distance to its own hub over the 0.8–5.5 s window. The term is dropped when
  the position is unknown.
* After all jumps are summed, each team's match total is multiplied by `opr_share_r^g_opr` (floored at
  0.02). The alliance is then renormalised to the same total, and every period's credit is scaled by the
  same per-team factor.
* The old rule is the special case `b = gz = 1`, `prior = 0.12`, `hidden = 2`, `g_dist = g_opr = 0`.
* **Fitting.** Positive constants are fitted in log space by L-BFGS. The loss is the cross-entropy of the
  match share against the scouted share, plus an L2 pull toward the old rule.
* **Cross-validation.** Leave-one-match-out: both alliances of a match are held out together, 54 folds.
  Inside each training fold, a grouped 5-fold CV picks the L2 strength from {0, 3e-5, 3e-4, 3e-3, 3e-2},
  so the held-out numbers are honest. Bootstrap intervals resample matches (2000 draws).

* `prior` 0.12 → 0.002: a robot in plain view that launches nothing gets essentially no credit.
* `hidden` 2.0 → 6.2 and `b` 1 → 0.60: evidence is compressed. A robot with 4× the launch evidence gets
  about 2.3× the weight, and a robot no camera sees keeps a real share. This fits launch detection
  saturating in volleys and noisy links.
* `gz` 0.76: the teleop zone gate is a little softer.
* `g_dist` −0.65 per 2 m: a robot 2 m further from its hub than another gets 0.52× its weight.
* `g_opr` 0.37: each team's match total is multiplied by its OPR share^0.37.

### Caveats

* **One event, one scouting team.** Everything was fitted on 104 alliances at a single event, scouted by
  one team. The L2 search picked almost no shrinkage, so the constants moved far from the old rule
  (`prior` 0.12 → 0.002). The 54-fold held-out numbers are the honest estimate. Re-fit the model when more
  scouting arrives:

      build_ds.py SNAPSHOT ds.pkl
      fit_cv.py ds.pkl --boot
      export_model.py ds.pkl attr_model.json --lam 3e-5 --cv cv.json

* **Shots, not makes.** The scouting reference counts shots, not makes. A team that shoots more
  accurately than its partners is under-weighted by the reference, not by the model.
* **The OPR prior is a team-level average.** It pulls a team's per-match share toward that average.
  Early in an event, or for a team that broke down in one match, the OPR is a poor guide. The strength
  (power 0.37) does not depend on how many matches the OPR is built from. The prior multiplies the video
  share, so a robot the video gives almost nothing (dead or absent) stays near zero.
* **Not affected:** alliances whose counter comes from TBA period totals instead of the overlay
  (2 of 262 here) use the same weights, and `split_nnls` is untouched.

**Findings**

* **Much less track fragmentation.**
  * Segments per match fall 29 % (285 → 202), and fewer in all 14 of 14 matches (ratio 0.52-0.90).
  * Segments are 28 % longer.
  * Steps with a phantom 4th robot per alliance halve (7.9 % → 3.7 %).
  * The cut is still ~24 % after allowing for the lower coverage.
  * Whole-robot v2 does not reduce fragmentation (306 segments).
* **Slightly more consistent positions.** End- and main-camera fixes of the same robot agree
  better: median 0.49 m vs 0.53 m.
* **Keep the 0.4 m push.** Colin's "bumper bottom-centre, no push" made cameras disagree more
  (0.66 m) and fragmentation worse (232 segments). A bumper box's bottom-centre is the robot's
  near edge, exactly like a whole-robot box's, so `ROBOT_HALF_DEPTH` applies to both.
* **Fewer robots found.** 13-15 % fewer detections, and coverage drops from 69 % to 64 %. In the

  finds, and some bumpers are boxed one face only.

  all lie within noise of whole-robot v1 (28 alliances).

  of detector does.

## End-camera calibration recovery: event prior + fixed-intrinsics fallback

**Problem.** In 7 of the 76 processed matches, the alliance's own end camera had no calibration
(failure-finder cycle 3h). Every box in that view then had no field position, so its launches could
not be credited. Three causes:

* the optimiser landing in a bad minimum with 4-7 tags in view (cancmp_qm114 f 3001 px / rms 7.9;
  cancmp_qm52 f 52 px / rms 43);
* only two coplanar tags in view (cagle_qm32, cagle_sf13m1: tags 5 and 8 on one face of the red hub);
* the camera aimed away from the tags (casnv_qm7: one tag; cc_qm15: 0-2 tags; joh_qm14: none).

**Result.** Six of the seven views are recovered. joh_qm14 never shows a tag and stays uncalibrated.
For the affected alliances:

* credited launches 423 -> 722;
* held-out identity 86.1 -> 88.7 %;
* share error vs fuel OPR 0.191 -> 0.174 on average, but only 3 of the 6 recovered alliances improve;
* on cc_qm15, agreement with 5026's scouting improves (team fuel MAE 72.4 -> 65.9, correlation
  0.77 -> 0.85).

Views that already calibrate are unchanged.

### What changed

`autoscout/field.py`

* **Event prior (a).** `update_calib_prior` / `load_calib_prior` keep
  `data/cache/calib_<event>_<view>.json`:
  * it stores each match's plain end-view fit (rms < 4, centre near the field) and the median K,
    distortion and camera centre over them, used once there are at least 3 matches;
  * recovered cameras are marked `cam.recovered` and never feed it.
  * The caches for all 5 events are seeded from the scans in `output/`. Per event and view this is
    12-18 matches; e.g. cancmp blue_end: f 687 px, k1 -0.307, centre (0.62, -0.13, 0.52) m.
* **Focal-length gate (b).** `calibrate_view_dynamic(..., prior=)` and `calibrate_view(..., prior=)`
  also start from the event K and reject any fit whose focal length is more than 30 % off the event
  median. With `prior=None` (the default) the code path is unchanged.
* **Fixed-intrinsics fallback (c).** `calibrate_view_fixed`. K and distortion are fixed to the
  event values, and one pose is solved per sampled frame (every 4 s, the same frames as
  `pipeline.calibrate`):
  * frames with 2 or more tags: `solvePnPRansac` drops outlier tags. Frames where at least 3 tags
    agree are "anchors".
  * every frame with at least 1 tag: a rotation about the camera centre (one tag is enough: 8
    equations, 3 unknowns), with a tag-level consensus. Each tag in turn proposes the rotation, and
    the proposal the most other tags agree with wins.
  * with 3 or more anchors, this match's camera centre and all rotations are refined jointly. The
    refine starts once from the event centre and once from the anchors' median centre, and keeps the
    start whose result has the lower truncated-quadratic tag cost.
  * isolated pose jumps are dropped, and poses are interpolated in time like any `CameraTrack`.
* `recover_view` returns the candidates: the event-K fit (if rms < 4) and the fixed-K track.

`autoscout/pipeline.py` (calibration section only)

* `calibrate(..., event=None, match=None, fallbacks=None)`. With `event` set:
  * every end-view fit is added to the prior;
  * a failed end view gets recovery candidates in `fallbacks`.
  * With the defaults it is unchanged.
* **Accept gate (d).** `cross_view_error` and `accept_recovered_cams`:
  * each robot box in the view is placed with the candidate camera exactly as `analyse()` places it;
  * the distance to the nearest same-alliance main-view robot at the same instant is measured;
  * a candidate passes on a median under 1 m over at least 30 boxes, and the best passing candidate
    replaces `md.cams[view]`.
* `process_match` is **not** wired yet: the main session asked for the default path to stay unchanged
  while its batch runs. To enable recovery in fresh scans, replace its `cams = calibrate(...)` line
  with:

  ```python
  recover = {}
  cams = calibrate(video, prod, tl.auto_start - 1, tele + config.TELEOP_LEN + 2, event=info.event_key,
                   match=key, fallbacks=recover)
  # ... after md = scan_video(...):
  if recover:
      accept_recovered_cams(md, recover)
  ```

Other files

* `scripts/recalibrate.py <scan_dir> <match>... | --missing`:
  * seeds the event priors from the scans of the same events, then re-runs only the missing end views
    from `data/videos/<match>.mp4`;
  * gates each candidate and saves with `pipeline._save_scan`, replacing `scan.pkl` atomically;
  * writes `calib_recovery.json` beside each recovered scan;
  * is idempotent: a second run leaves every scan byte-identical.
* `tests/test_core.py`: 2 synthetic tests, for pan poses with a misplaced tag and for the prior's
  median and exclusions. All 16 tests pass.

## Cross-match DINOv2 robot recognition for team identity (2026-10-18)

**Question.** The 2026-10-17 DINO attempt (PLAN.md section 11) trained a classifier inside one match and made
identity worse: it learned where a robot was, not which robot. Each team plays ~10-12 matches per event,
so a classifier trained on a team's crops from its *other* matches sees it against many backgrounds and
positions. Does that recognise robots, and does it improve identity?

**Answer.** It recognises robots very well. Plugged into today's identity model, it barely moves held-out
identity, because the remaining errors are mostly duplicate tracks. The 6-permutation Viterbi cannot label
those, however sure the evidence is. Combined with the duplicate-capable Viterbi from `data/exp_identity`
(`identity.assign_absent`), the probe takes held-out identity from **91.2 % to 97.4 %**, and every event
improves.

* **Probe accuracy.** Leave-one-match-out on Chezy, the probe names the right team of the alliance for
  **99.3 % of OCR-confident segments** and **97.3 % of single crops** (97.2 % in the high main camera).
* **Background.** Handling it barely matters once training spans matches. The whole-box CLS token scores
  96.5 % of crops, the box-interior patch mean 97.3 %, and the robot-pixel mask 97.0 %.
* **Probe alone.** As an extra evidence term in the current 6-permutation Viterbi, the probe moves held-out
  identity only from 91.15 to **91.71 %** (`w_dino=2`). ResNet galleries reach 91.40 %.
* **Why so little.** On Chezy, the probe already votes for the team named by the held-out bumper reads in
  11.6 of the 12.8 points of error.
  * In 11.4 of those points, a concurrent segment of the same alliance holds that team.
  * In 9.4 points, that concurrent segment *also* looks like that team. These are two tracks of one robot,
    and the permutation model has to give the second track another team.
* **With the duplicate-capable Viterbi.** Using `absent_cost=2` for every team, the probe resolves those
  duplicates: **97.42 %** (Chezy 89.5 -> **98.7 %**). The same model without the probe gives 94.00 %.
* **Against 5026's scouting** (315 team-matches):
  * The video-only split improves: fuel MAE 51.5 -> 48.8, correlation 0.808 -> 0.825.
  * We refit the learned credit model on the new identities with nested leave-one-match-out CV. All four
    scouting metrics improve slightly: MAE 44.6 -> 44.3, share error 0.135 -> 0.131, share correlation
    0.826 -> 0.841, top scorer 62 -> 68 %.
  * The shipped `models/attr_model.json` was fitted on the *current* identities of these same matches.
    With it unchanged, the new identities score worse in-sample (MAE 42.7 -> 46.8), so the credit model
    must be refit together with any identity change.

### Method (`scripts/build_dino_probe.py`, tools in `data/exp_dino/tools/`)

* **embed**
  * Re-reads the crop frames from `data/videos/<match>.mp4` and embeds every stored robot crop with frozen
    DINOv2 ViT-S/14 on the GPU: 671 k crops in 131 matches.
  * Each crop is embedded in 9 crop / pooling strategies at once, at 224 px (the 96 px thumbnails are too
    small for end-camera close-ups).
  * Takes 25 s per match (decode-bound), and writes 35 MB per match to `data/exp_dino/emb/` (4.4 GB).
* **labels**
  * Per crop: its segment, alliance, assigned team and the segment's bumper-OCR evidence.
  * Comes from the finalized `output/<match>/result.json` + `dets.json` (default Options).
* **probe**
  * Per event and leave-one-match-out: a softmax probe over the event's teams (L-BFGS, L2 1e-3,
    standardised features). It is trained on the crops of *other* matches' confident segments, which have
    OCR mass >= 2, >= 80 % on one team, and agree with the assigned team (the `build_galleries.py` rule).
  * Crops are weighted 1/sqrt(segment length), with classes balanced.
  * For the held-out match it stores per crop the log-probabilities over each alliance's 3 teams. Teams the
    probe never saw share the mass it gives to "none of the alliance's known teams", and probabilities are
    floored at 0.02.
  * Output goes to `data/exp_dino/probe/<name>/<match>.npz`. A match's own OCR never trains its probe.
* **pipeline**
  * `Options.w_dino` (default 0 = off) adds `w_dino x` the centred mean per-crop log q of each segment to
    the OCR evidence before the first Viterbi. It gets full weight from 5 crops on.
  * The probe files are read from `pipeline.DINO_PROBE_DIR` (default `data/exp_dino/probe/default`, a copy
    of `box_inner_ocr`).
* **evaluation**
  * `data/exp_dino/tools/eval_ident.py` uses the same metric as `eval_analysis.py --holdout`. It is faster:
    each scan is loaded once for all configs, held-out runs stop after identity, and bumper reads are cached
    in `data/exp_dino/ocr_cache/`.
  * Its base number equals `identity_holdout.py` from finalize_v3 exactly (91.15 %).
  * `scripts/eval_analysis.py` got an optional `--ocr_cache DIR`, which gives the same results about 3x
    faster.

## Process of elimination for unidentified robots and launches (2026-10-19, adopted)

**Idea (Bruce).** When the other robots of an alliance are seen somewhere else, an unidentified robot, or an
unclaimed launch, must belong to the remaining one.

**Why it was needed.** In 2026cc_qm17, 6800 is the blue alliance's top scorer. Scouts count about 350 shots;
it "dumps a full hopper in 3-4 s" from right under the blue end camera. It still got 0 linked launches, for
two reasons:
* **Mislabelled track.** The causal DINO probe had barely seen 6800, so it called 6800's track "6665" at full
  confidence. The identity Viterbi then labelled two tracks metres apart "6665" for 30 s and marked 6800
  absent.
* **No box at all.** 6800 sits too close to the camera to be boxed, so its arcs had no box and no tracked
  robot near them, and were dropped.

6800's 286 credited balls came entirely from the credit model's hidden-robot pseudo-shots. Meanwhile 6665,
pure defence and 0 shots, had 43 launches pinned on it.

**What changed** (`autoscout/pipeline.py`, `identity.assign_absent`):

| piece | switch | rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tracks | `IDENT_FAR_COST = 20` (`IDENT_FAR_M = 2.5`) | Two concurrent tracks of one alliance more than 2.5 m apart pay 20 per epoch to share a label. The mirror image of the co-located duplicate bonus. |
| "?" boxes | `ELIM_BOXES` | A box with no field position (image edge, far down an end camera) gets the one team of its alliance not labelled on another box in the frame and not tracked elsewhere (projects more than `ELIM_FAR` = 1.5 box widths away). |
| box-less arcs | `ELIM_ARCS` | A clean rising arc with no box or tracked robot near it, launched inside an alliance zone, goes to that alliance's one robot not tracked more than 2 m from the launch point. Confidence 0.6, `how: "elim"`. |
| with them | `BOX_HOLD_S = 1.5`, `LINK_FILL_TIE = 3` | From the visual QA (`data/exp_visual_qa/report.md`). A segment's last box stays a link candidate for 1.5 s after the detector drops it. Between overlapping boxes, the robot whose hopper fill drops across the launch wins. |

## Hub entry times and the scoreboard lag (2026-10-18; re-timing built, credit change not adopted)

**Question.** Each scoreboard jump is shared among the alliance's robots by their launch arcs 0.8-5.5 s before it
(`attribution.kernel`). Bruce saw in the inspector that the delay between a ball going in and the counter moving
is wide and variable. If we knew when each counted ball dropped into the hub, could a narrow per-ball match to
the launch one flight time earlier replace the broad window and credit the right robot more often?

**Answer.** The delay is now understood and measurable per ball, but crediting from it does not pay off yet.

* **Why the lag varies.** The hub counts at most about 15 balls/s. A ball that drops into an idle hub is
  counted about 1.3 s later. In a hopper dump the balls queue, and the last ones are counted 2-4 s after they
  went in. Between matches and broadcasts the idle delay is stable (spread 0.2-0.4 s).
* **Entry times.** Balls dropping into the opening (flight-track ends plus blobs over the opening) re-time every
  counted ball, first in first out. This halves the timing spread when checked against a camera view the
  re-timing did not use (IQR 1.53 s to 0.72 s).
* **Arcs.** Only 1.6 % of counted balls can be followed along one tracked arc from a linked launch into the
  hub (6 % with loose thresholds). The launch-to-entry link therefore has to be statistical, by timing.
* **Credit.**
  * The best variant (half narrow entry kernel, half today's kernel) lowers video-only team-match error by
    1.0 ball (95 % CI −1.8 .. −0.1).
  * With the credit model refitted fairly for every arm, the best-estimate error drops 0.5 balls
    (−1.3 .. +0.2), which is within noise.
  * Per-jump agreement with the scouts drops 0.8 points (−1.0 .. −0.5).
  * 254 loses more volleys to teammates: 177 balls against 152.
  * Narrower variants trade more per-jump agreement for slightly lower match error.
* **The limit is launch recall.** About 0.22 launches are detected per counted ball, so a narrow window often
  holds no launch from the real shooter. With half the launches dropped, the gain disappears.
* **Recommendation: don't adopt the credit change now.** Keep `autoscout/hubentry.py` for diagnostics, fix the
  lag description in the docs, and re-run the A/B once the tracker's launch recall improves (commands at the end).

**In plain words.** The scoreboard is not just late, it is a queue. The hub lets about 15 balls a second
past its sensor. When a robot dumps 60 balls in two seconds, the scoreboard keeps ticking for another four or
five. We can now tell from the video when each ball actually went in. But we see only about one launch in five,
so pinning each ball on the launch exactly one flight before it is often a guess. Today's wide window, which
pools several seconds of launches, is about as good. The sharper timing will start to pay once more launches
are detected.

### 2. Entry detection (`autoscout/hubentry.py`, `tools/extract.py`, `entry_quality.py`, `retime_quality.py`)

**Entry tracks.** A flight track counts as an entry when its last point lies in the projected hub opening:
* |u − c_u| ≤ 1.0 r and −0.6 r ≤ v − c_v ≤ 1.2 r, where r is the opening's image radius at the current camera pose;
* and it is moving down at the end.

The track ends of all 131 matches cluster tightly there (main and own-end views). The far end view is useless.

**Entry evidence.** `entry_series` sums four sources, each scaled to mean 1 over the match: entry tracks and
blobs over the opening (shifted +0.2 s), in the main view and the alliance's own end view.

**Re-timing.** `retime_queue` works per counter burst; a gap > 1 s means the queue emptied.
* The burst's first ball entered 0.8-2.6 s before its count.
* No ball entered before the previous burst's last ball.
* Cumulative counted balls are matched to cumulative evidence above background. Each read's balls are cut into
  4 pieces.
* Delays are clipped to [0.6, 7] s.
* A burst with too little evidence falls back to count time − 1.35 s.

The ball count is exactly the counter's.

| | Chezy | FIRST |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| entry tracks per counted ball: main / own end / far end view | 0.47 / 0.52 / 0.34 | 0.81 / 0.68 / 0.23 |
| corr. of an alliance-match's entry-track count with its counted balls (main / own end / far) | 0.81 / 0.68 / 0.06 | 0.81 / 0.58 / −0.05 |
| entry tracks that are themselves linked launch arcs | 4 % | 4-5 % |

| ... IR mass within ±0.5 s of its peak (raw counter → constant shift 1.6 s → re-timed) | 0.39 → 0.39 → 0.65 | 0.45 → 0.45 → 0.59 |

Counting balls from entries alone would be poor (0.5-0.8 tracks per ball, varying by match). This is why the
vision-only counter is ~30 % off. Timing is what entries are good for. Against a view the re-timing never saw,
the re-timed balls line up at 0 s with half the spread of the raw counter. A constant shift does not help at
all; only the per-ball queue model does.

### 3. Entries to arcs to robots (`tools/arc_link.py`)

An entry track is linked to a robot in one of two ways:
* **direct:** it is a launch arc that `analyse` already linked;
* **chained:** a linked launch arc in the same view ended ≤ 1.2 s before it, and one projectile (u linear, v
  quadratic) fits both fragments within 4 px RMS, with a consistent speed.

Links are deduplicated across views.

| | Chezy | FIRST |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| counted balls with a confident arc (direct + chained) | **1.6 %** (660 + 426 of 68,563) | **1.9 %** (392 + 328 of 38,305) |
| ... with loose thresholds (10 px, 2 s gap) | 5.8 % | 10.3 % |
| linked arcs whose robot is of the hub's alliance | 97 % (loose 95 %) | 95 % (93 %) |
| flight time of fully linked arcs, q10 / median / q90 | 0.87 / 1.10 / 1.33 s | 0.80 / 1.07 / 1.30 s |

The links that exist are right: 97 % come from the hub's own alliance. They are just rare. A launch fragment
is usually 4-6 points (0.1-0.2 s) and the ball is lost near the apex, so almost no ball is followed from robot
to hub. Linking has to go through timing (launch + ~1.0 s flight ≈ entry), which is what the credit variants do.

## Dead, absent and parked robots: absent identity state and parked-robot PRIOR (failure-finder cycle 16 follow-up)

**Question.** Robots the scouts saw doing nothing (dead, absent, parked, defence only) got 0.13-0.31 of their
alliance's fuel. Cycle 16 found two mechanisms: (1) `identity.assign` must use all three team labels, so a
never-read dead robot's label lands on a teammate's duplicate track and takes its launches (2026cc_qm18: the
box labelled 3045 shows "6647"); (2) a visible robot that sits still in its zone collects PRIOR on every
scoreboard jump (qm23 4499: 79 % of its credit was PRIOR).

### How it was measured

`pipeline.analyse` in memory on every `output/<match>/scan.pkl` (131 matches; nothing written to `output/`),
all variants of a match on one scan load, so every comparison is paired
(`data/exp_identity/tools/absent_eval.py`, metrics `summarize.py`, robot table `cases.py`; runs in `runs/`).

* **Bumper reads missing from the scans.** `reanalyse` makes new reads on the GPU and never saves them into
  `scan.pkl`: 46.5k crops (~350 per match) are uncached, and EasyOCR on the CPU takes ~2.3 s per crop. Each
  segment's evidence missing from the cache is taken from the production `result.json` (`identity[..]["ocr"]`)
  and spread over that segment's uncached crops, so the normal analysis reproduces production identity
  (0 of 131 matches with a segmentation mismatch; base fuel within 0.5 of `result.json` for 127 of 131 matches).
  The held-out identity split of that injected evidence (by crop id parity) is approximate.
* **Scouting** (5026, Chezy quals): 104 alliances with all three robots scouted; 32 of them have a non-shooter
  (scouted share < 0.06, every scout <= 15 shots; cycle 16b's definition). Error = within-alliance share
  total variation (TV), mean over alliances; corr = pooled share correlation; MAE = fuel vs TBA total split by
  scouted shots (`score_scouting.py`'s reference).
* **Held-out identity**: `eval_analysis.py --holdout` logic (labels from half the reads, scored on the other half).
* **Fuel OPR**: pooled within-alliance share corr, 259 alliances (train = casnv + joh); at its noise floor.
* The main session changed `pipeline.py`, `overlay.py` and the scans during this work, so the base differs
  between runs; compare variants only within a run. Final run = code of 02:27, 18 Oct.

### Findings

1. **The absent state fixes identity, not credit.** It relabels the duplicate tracks the reads name (held-out
   identity +0.3 pp, CI excludes 0), but the forced label was not the main source of a dead robot's fuel. A
   robot that is not there is usually *hidden* in the model (its label rides an inactive slot), and hidden
   robots earn HIDDEN credit; the identity model has no evidence that tells "hidden" from "not there"
   (6036: 75 % of its credit was HIDDEN, only 5 s became absent).
2. **The dead-robot flag cannot tell dead from unread.** In the scouted alliances it flags 2 dead robots
   (3045, 6036) and 6 robots that did shoot (scouted share 0.07-0.43) but whose bumpers were never read (2910,
   9496, 6017, 1540, 5507, 581). Their labelled segments carry teammates' reads just as often (probably
   misreads or a neighbour's bumper in the crop). Relabelling those segments costs 2910 0.10 and 9496 0.05 of
   share, which cancels the gain on 3045 and 6036.
   Zeroing HIDDEN for flagged teams (`absent_dead_hidden_s`) takes 3045 to 0.05 but 9496 to 0.11 (scouted
   0.39) and lowers OPR corr by 0.007: rejected. Per-team visible time and co-location with teammates
   separate 6036 from the rest, but with one example that would be a fitted rule.
3. **Parked robots are easy to see and safe to fix.** The median 20 s spread of a robot's observed positions is
   < 1.0 m for 13 of 786 team-matches, 1.0-1.5 m for 3 and 1.5-3.5 m for 8 (all unscouted, cagle / casnv /
   cancmp); the 5th percentile is 4.5 m. In the scouted cc quals the parked cluster (<= 0.94 m) is separated
   from everyone else (>= 3.9 m). All 5 scouted parked non-shooters lose credit; the one parked robot that did
   shoot (qm38 9128, 40 shots while standing still, mostly hidden) keeps its HIDDEN and launches and moves
   0.01. Zeroing HIDDEN as well cost it 0.08 (prototype), so `park_ext` removes PRIOR only. Launches linked to
   a parked robot stay, which is why qm23 4499 keeps 0.07.
4. Remaining over-credit of non-shooters is launch evidence linked to the wrong robot (qm17 6665 defence,
   qm12), not identity or PRIOR.

### Recommendation

* **Adopt the parked-robot flag, `Options.park_ext = 1.0`.** At 1.5 (measured on all 131 matches) scouting
  improves on every non-shooter it touches (5 better, 0 worse), share TV -0.0009 [-0.0021, -0.0001] on all 104
  alliances, OPR unchanged (0.643 -> 0.644). 1.0 gives the same scouting result and leaves out the three
  unscouted robots at 1.0-1.3 m that nothing validates.
* **Absent state: optional, not for fuel.** `absent_cost_dead = 2.0, absent_near_bonus = 2.0` raises held-out
  identity by 0.3 pp and is neutral on fuel share (scouting and OPR); turn it on if per-team paths/identity
  matter. It needs a better dead-vs-unread signal before it helps attribution.
* Keep `absent_dead_hidden_s` and `absent_cost` (non-dead teams) off.

## Launch-to-robot link between side-by-side robots (2026-10-18, adopted)

**Problem.** Bruce saw 254's volleys credited to a teammate in the inspector. In 2026cc_qm24 at 12.4 s, 254 is
shooting in the blue end camera, but most launch arcs went to 5507 next to it. A launch is tied to the robot
box it rises from. Each box is widened by 25 % on either side, so neighbouring boxes overlap. When an arc
starts inside two widened boxes, the tie was broken only by which box top it was nearest, never by left or
right. The taller neighbour won even when the ball rose out of the middle of 254's box.

**Fix.** The tie-break now also adds |u − box centre| / box width (`pipeline.LINK_X_TIE = 1`). Here u is
where the arc, traced back along its initial velocity, crossed that box's top edge (`LINK_X_CROSS`). Balls
drift sideways towards the hub as they rise, so the first point seen can sit over the neighbour.

with every variant and scored against 5026's scouting with `scripts/score_scouting.py`. The credit model and

## Main-camera calibration at Chezy Champs (failure-finder cycle 8)

**Problem.** 30 % of the Chezy (2026cc) main-view robot boxes had no field position, so they got no track,
team or credit. Other events had 0.1-1.1 %. Within a match the off-field share swung from 0 to 0.89 per 4 s
pose window, while every window fitted its 4 tags to 0.5 px.

**Root cause: a Rodrigues-branch bug, not the tags.** The Chezy main camera looks back across the field from
high in the stands, so its rotation is close to 180 deg (|rvec| 2.96-3.34 at cc; 1.1-2.1 for every other main
view). Near 180 deg every rotation has two rotation vectors, r and r(1 - 2pi/|r|).

* The per-window fits returned either branch: consecutive windows switched branch 8-22 times per match in
  19 of 23 matches.
* `CameraTrack.at` blended neighbouring windows linearly, so between two windows on different branches the
  pose was garbage and the floor points landed tens of metres away.
* The four matches without a single switch (qm3, qm7, qm15, qm30) are exactly the four with ~0 off-field
  boxes.

**Two smaller, real problems from the tags.** The main view sees only tags 2, 11, 21 and 24. They lie in one
plane at one height, ~12 px wide, 20-30 m away.

* Each window's own fit puts the camera centre somewhere else: the window centres scatter 0.45-1.56 m around
  their median at cc, against at most 0.36 m at any other event.
* Focal length trades against distance: per-match fits give f 1800-3000 px and centre y 25-38 m.
* The camera is physically the same in every cc match: the tags sit at the same pixels to within 3 px and the
  same width.

### What changed

`autoscout/field.py`: one local edit, already live.

* `CameraTrack.at` now blends on the first pose's branch: before blending, r1 is moved to its other branch if
  that is closer to r0.
* Where consecutive rvecs are already on one branch the result is bitwise identical. Checked on a 0.1 s grid
  for all 48 non-cc cameras of the 16 non-cc matches below: 0 difference.
* In all of `output/` (92 scans) it changes only views that actually switch branch:
  * 2026cc main views;
  * 7 cc end views (e.g. qm28 blue_end, 10 switches);
  * one non-cc camera, cancmp_qm44 blue_end: one window has |rvec| 7.9, which is the 2pi-wrapped twin of
    1.6.
* Every saved scan gets this on a plain reanalyse. No rescan or recalibration is needed.

`autoscout/maincal.py` (new):

* `fit_static`: one focal length, k1 and camera centre for every window of a match, with one rotation per
  window (a pan/tilt head may turn but does not move). It is a robust least-squares fit on all windows' tag
  corners, started from the stored fit and from the planar-PnP centre (`ippe_start`, `cv2.solvePnPGeneric`
  `SOLVEPNP_IPPE`; physically impossible solutions are dropped). Output rvecs are kept on one branch
  (`unwrap_rvecs`).
* Optional terms, used in the experiments only:
  * a floor term (robot boxes must land on the field);
  * a far-wall term;
  * fixed landmark points.
* `static_from_prior`: the event K, distortion and centre held fixed, with a rotation per window about that
  centre.
* `load_main_prior` / `update_main_prior` store the event prior in `data/cache/calib_<event>_main.json`:
  * each match's static fit is recorded there;
  * the prior is their median (via `field.load_calib_prior`, >= 3 matches, the match itself excluded).
  * A hand-checked camera in `calib_<event>_main_landmarks.json`, if present, is "trusted" (see below).
* `guard`, the safety net:
  * a window pose that puts >= 20 % of its own boxes off the field is dropped, and its time is interpolated
    from the neighbouring poses;
  * a match is flagged when more than half of its poses are bad.
* `centre_scatter`: the trigger. Nothing new happens unless the stored window centres scatter by more than
  0.4 m. Over all 92 scans in `output/`, the largest non-cc value is 0.36 m (cagle_qm27) and the smallest cc
  value is 0.45 m (qm17).

`autoscout/pipeline.py`

* `calibrate()`: for a main view over the trigger, it builds the static fit and, if an event prior exists, the
  prior's rotation-only track. The static fit is recorded in the prior. The candidates go into
  `fallbacks["main"]`.
* `accept_main_cam(md, candidates, prefer, trusted)` is the post-scan gate, called by `process_match` before
  the end-view gate. Every candidate is first cleaned by `guard`. Pass rules:
  * **Normal candidate:** the share of main boxes off the field must drop by more than 0.002, and the end
    views' mean `cross_view_error` median may rise by at most 0.02 m.
  * **Preferred candidate** (the event-prior track) also passes if the share does not rise (beyond 0.002).
    This deviates from "must drop": the event prior is there to make the cameras consistent across matches,
    which also matters where nothing is off the field.
  * **Trusted landmark camera:** the cross-view check is only reported, not enforced.
  * The report is kept as `md.main_calib`; a flag is printed.
* The flag `MAIN_STATIC = True` turns all of this off when set to False.

`scripts/recalibrate_main.py <scan_dir> <match>... | --event EV`:

* **Pass 1:** the main tags are detected again from `data/videos/<match>.mp4`, at the same frames as
  `calibrate`. The static fit is made and recorded in the event prior.
* **Pass 2:** the same candidates and gate as fresh scans.
* Saving: `pipeline._save_scan` writes to a temporary folder, then `os.replace` puts it in place, with
  `maincal_recovery.json` beside it.
* A second run leaves fixed scans byte-identical (checked by md5).
* Options: `--dry-run` (it still records the static fits in the prior cache), `--landmarks FILE`,
  `--no-prior`, `--force`.

Tests (`tests/test_maincal.py`, 4 tests) cover:

* blending across branches;
* the static fit;
* the guard;
* the gate, including that a camera with nothing off the field is left alone.

`tests/`: 34 passed.

## Two-view launch points: which robot launched a ball, from two cameras at once (2026-10-18, not adopted)

**Question.** A launch arc is tied to the robot box it rises from in one view. Teammates side by side get the
right credit only about 65 % of the time, and a robot hidden behind another one (254 behind 3847 in the Chezy blue
end camera) has no box at all. A ball seen in two views at the same instants is pinned down in 3-D, and every robot
has a fused field position. Does rebuilding the arc in 3-D, following it back to launch height and taking the
robot standing there beat today's single-view link?

**Answer.** No, not with today's calibrations. The 3-D rebuild is correct (exact on synthetic balls), but the three
Chezy views disagree about points in the air by roughly 0.1-0.5 m.

* **Why it fails.** True cross-view pairs fit at 7-15 px instead of the ~2-3 px the tracks allow, so they are
  hard to tell from chance pairs between unrelated tracks. For launches first seen in an end camera (most of
  them), launch points land a median 1.4-1.6 m from the scouted shooter, while the fit itself claims ±0.15 m.
* **Coverage.** 83 % of launch tracks have some cross-view candidate, but most of those are chance pairs. 3.0 % of
  launch tracks (5.4 % of credited launches) have a partner that fits both views to 6 px, and 13 % (22 %) to 10 px.
  Almost all pairs are an end camera with the main camera; the two end cameras share almost no balls (< 1 %).
* **Picks** (scout-judged truth sets of `data/exp_arcs`, today's rule 71.4 %, same-alliance 61.3 %):
  * Taking the two-view robot outright is worse wherever it covers much: −1.0 pp (10 px rule) to −3.3 pp (any fit).
  * Only the strictest rule is neutral: +0.2 pp (CI −0.3 .. +0.8), 4.5 % of cases.
  * As a soft term added to today's score (λ by leave-one-match-out), +0.7 pp (CI +0.1 .. +1.4) on c24 and +0.5 pp
    (−0.1 .. +1.1) on the fresh set. That is a quarter of what the single-view arc check gained (+3.0).
* **Hidden robots.** It does reach robots with no box: on the 1,028 judged launches whose shooter had no box in
  the view, it credits the right one in up to 17 % (today: 0 %). It loses as many elsewhere, so the launches with
  a teammate behind the 2-D winner stay at 59.9 → 58.4-60.6 % (CIs span 0).
* **Fuel vs 5026's scouting** (54 scouted Chezy quals, paired by match): neither arm moves per-team fuel.
  * Strict relink (6 px): video-only MAE +0.08 (CI −0.23 .. +0.40).
  * Soft term (λ = 0.01): −0.08 (−0.33 .. +0.17).
  * 254's burst check is unchanged in both.
* **Recommendation: don't adopt.** Nothing to port. `autoscout/triangulate.py` stays an experimental module.
  The precondition for another try is a consistent 3-D calibration of all three views (below).

**In plain words.** Two cameras looking at the same ball should say exactly where it is. Here they disagree by
about a ball-to-robot distance, because the cameras' positions are only known well along the floor, not in the
air. With that much disagreement the method can't reliably tell which ball in one view is which ball in the
other, and when it does pair them the launch point is about 1.5 m off. So it picks the shooter no better than
today's single-camera rules. A better camera fit makes it clearly better, but still not good enough.

### Method (`autoscout/triangulate.py`, tools in `data/exp_multiview/tools/`)

* **Geometry.**
  * The three views share one frame grid: the fuel scan runs every 2nd frame for the whole broadcast frame, so
    all views have the same timestamps.
  * Each track point becomes a normalised image point (`cv2.undistortPoints`) and a world ray. The pose at its
    own time comes from `CameraTrack.at(t)`, and the intrinsics are fixed per view.
* **`MultiView.candidates(va, i, vb)`.**
  * Tracks of view vb seen at ≥ 3 of the anchor's instants.
  * Median closest distance between the two views' rays ≤ 1 m.
  * Both depths positive, and the midpoints inside the field box (z −0.3 .. 8 m). At most 12 per view.
* **`MultiView.fit_pair` → `fit_linear`.** A closed-form ballistic fit.
  * X(s) = P0 + V0 s − g s²/2. Each sighting gives two equations linear in (P0, V0): the projection constraint.
  * Each equation is weighted by f / depth, so residuals are image px, and re-weighted 4 times (soft-L1, 3 px).
  * It uses the anchor's points up to the end of the overlap + 0.1 s and the partner's inside the overlap ± 0.1 s.
  * About 0.3 ms per fit, ~80 times faster than `least_squares`. The answer is the same to a few px (`fit_path`
    is kept as the reference).
* **`launch_point`.**
  * The earlier time the path is at 0.55 m (rising, vz > 0.3 m/s, at most 1 s before or 0.3 s after the first
    sighting), and the field point there.
  * Covariance = the fit's parameter covariance (σ² = robust residual variance, floor 1.5 px) pushed through by
    finite differences, plus a launch-height spread of 0.2 m along the path.
* **`pick_robot`.**
  * Mahalanobis² of each robot's fused position under the launch covariance + (0.45 m)² I.
  * The pick must pass χ²(2) 99 % (9.21) and beat the next robot by 2.
  * Robots need no box in any view: the A/B uses the fused team tracks (`team_arr`), the truth sets the logged
    `xy_fused` of each candidate.
* **Zero-context.** Each fit uses only that match's own tracks, camera poses and robot tracks. The constants are
  global and were not tuned per event. The landmark camera is the only exception, and it is used as a
  diagnostic only.
* **Passes.**
  1. `mv_pairs.py`: every candidate pair of every launch track, one scan at a time, read and closed at once.
     About 15 s per match. Output `pairs/<match>.pkl`, or `pairs_lm/` with `--lm`.
  2. `eval_mv.py`, `eval_soft.py`, `eval_same.py`, `eval_hidden_mv.py`, `coverage.py`, `diag_*.py`: offline,
     from the pairs and the `data/exp_arcs` logs.
  3. `ab_mv.py`: in-memory A/B (above). About 13 s per arm per match, CPU only.
* **Constants not tuned to the truth sets.** The acceptance rules are a small fixed grid; σ_robot, the gate and
  the margin were set before scoring. λ of the soft term is chosen by leave-one-match-out.

### What would make it work: one consistent 3-D calibration of the three views

**How good it must be.** Side-by-side robots stand ~0.9-1.2 m apart (centre to centre). With a launch-point error
σ along the line between them, the pick is right Φ(0.5 m / σ) of the time:

| σ | 0.25 m | 0.35 m | 0.5 m | today's two-view (heavy-tailed, median ~1.5 m) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| pick right | 98 % | 92 % | 84 % | ~65 % on covered contested cases |

To clearly beat today's 61 % same-alliance, σ should be ≤ 0.35 m in total. The fused robot position already costs
~0.2-0.3 m of that, so the cameras must agree on a point in the air to ~0.1-0.15 m:

* the two views' rays meet within ~0.1 m at common instants;
* joint-fit residuals of ~3-4 px in the main view (70-85 px/m at 20-25 m) and ~3-5 px in an end view;
* long pairs (≥ 6 instants) fitting at ≤ 4 px for most end-view launch tracks that have a candidate, far above the
  7 s time-shift chance level.

Today the views agree to ~0.2-0.5 m: 11-14 px median on long pairs. The landmark main camera alone gets the end
views to 7 px and the median launch error from 1.4-1.6 to 0.9-1.1 m, so the end cameras need fixing too.

**What it would take** (automatic, per match, zero-context):

1. **Unknowns.**
   * Per end camera: focal length, k1 and a pose (8; these cameras barely move).
   * Main camera: one focal length, k1 and centre, plus a rotation per 4 s window, as `maincal.fit_static`.
   * About 25-30 numbers per match beyond the main camera's window rotations.
2. **Constraints.** Each kind fixes what the others can't:
   * **Tags**, as today. They fix each view's image of its tags but not the main camera's focal length,
     distance and tilt (four coplanar tags at one height).
   * **Floor.** Robot box bottoms from different views must land on the same carpet point at the same instant.
     That ties the views together on the ground (the `maincal` floor term, but between views, not just "on the
     field").
   * **Points above the floor.**
     * Balls dropping into a hub vanish at the opening (1.83 m). On qm24 and qm1 their track ends cluster
       0.2-0.35 m below the projected opening centre in every view (`tools/hub_ends.py`). That is a free, nearly
       known 3-D landmark in all three views.
     * The hub's top corners and the tower uprights would serve too, if detected automatically.
   * **Shared ball tracks.** These are the strongest constraint on the air geometry. Each long cross-view pair adds
     2 × (instants) − 6 equations, and gravity fixes scale and the vertical. A match has ~250 end-view launch
     tracks with a ≥ 6-instant main candidate.
3. **Solve.** Robust bundle adjustment:
   * `scipy.optimize.least_squares`, soft-L1, sparse Jacobian. Unknowns are the camera parameters plus 6 path
     numbers per ball.
   * Pairing is re-done as the cameras improve (EM / RANSAC): start from long, mutual-best pairs only; refit;
     re-pair with a tighter threshold; repeat. Chance pairs get down-weighted by the robust loss and dropped at
     the next re-pairing.
4. **Checks.**
   * The time-shift null (`selfcal_test.py --shift`) must stay flat while real ≤ 4 px pairs multiply.
   * Robots re-fused with the new cameras must keep their cross-view agreement and reach the far wall (the
     `data/exp_maincal` test).
   * Then rerun `mv_pairs.py` → `eval_mv.py` / `eval_soft.py` unchanged.
5. **Cost.** A new module of a few hundred lines, and ~1-2 min of CPU per match for the adjustment. The
   per-match local search tried here (8 parameters on the main camera only, end cameras fixed) shows that a
   small correction is not enough: it must move focal length and distance together and include the end
   cameras.

* **The robots' fused positions must come from the same corrected cameras**, or the launch point and the robot are
  in different frames.
* **Recall still caps it.** In the qm24 window the volley's launches are missing before any linking (tracker work).

## Season-to-date fuel prior for the credit model

**Question.** With no hindsight, the learned credit model's fuel-OPR prior uses only the quals already played
at the event. The first match has no prior at all, and the next ones have a noisy one. Every team's earlier
events of the season are public before an event starts. Can those events fill the gap, alone or blended with
the in-event OPR, and does that lower the credit model's error against 5026's Chezy scouting?

**Answer.** Yes for the blend, which is recommended for adoption. With the blend, fuel MAE drops from 43.6 to
41.4 balls (nested CV). The 95 % interval of the difference, −4.2 … −0.3, excludes zero. Share error (TV) goes
from 0.129 to 0.124, with an interval of −0.010 … +0.001.

* **Where the gain comes from.** Almost all of it is in quals 21+ (43.8 → 40.8). On quals 1–20 the blend is
  42.5 against 43.2, too few matches to tell apart. The in-order prior's early gap to the hindsight
  full-event OPR is +1.0 balls here (43.2 vs 42.2); the blend closes about 0.7 of it.
* **Beyond hindsight.** The blend also beats the full-event OPR (42.4), which a live system cannot have.
  Ten quals per team is not much data, and the earlier events add about 7 more quals' worth per team.
* **The earlier events alone.** The season rate without any in-event data (42.6) is between the two and not
  distinguishable from the in-order prior.
* **Coverage.** All 42 Chezy teams have earlier 2026 events with TBA hub totals: 3–7 events each, a median of
  7.2 recency-weighted quals. With the in-order prior, 14 of the 105 scouted alliances have no prior, because
  a team had not played yet. With the blend, none do.

### Method (`autoscout/season.py`)

* **`season_rate(team, event_key)`.** The team's fuel OPR at each earlier event of the season, computed with
  `opr.fuel_opr` over that event's full quals from TBA hub totals.
  * Only events that ended before `event_key`'s start date count. Preseason events (type 100) and events
    with fewer than 8 scored quals are skipped.
  * Each event is weighted by quals played × 0.5^(days before the event / 60).
* **`prior_oprs(event_key, before=match_key)`.** This is the drop-in for `opr.fuel_opr(event, before=)`. It
  is a ridge OPR over the event's quals played before the match, shrunk toward each team's season rate.
  * The shrink strength is 0.5 alliance-match observations per earlier qual (`SEASON_WEIGHT`).
  * One least-squares factor, fitted to the alliance totals played so far, puts the season rates on the
    event's scale.
  * Before the first match, it returns the season rates themselves.
  * A team with no season rate gets the event mean, with `opr.fuel_opr`'s own ridge (0.5).
* **`season_prior(team, event_key, before_match)`.** One team's entry of `prior_oprs`.
* **Statbotics.** The v3 API returned HTTP 500 (once 503) for 2026 `team_year`, `team_event` and `year`,
  and for 2025, on both tries during this work. `statbotics_rate` is wired in as a fallback for teams with
  no TBA history. It is cached in `data/cache/statbotics` and returns None on any failure. Its 2026 field
  names are unverified, and Chezy never needed it.
* **Caching.** TBA responses are cached by `tba.get` in `data/cache/tba`. A cold start at Chezy needs 42
  `team_events` calls plus 69 event match lists, about 2 minutes at the rate TBA served today.

### Removed from the default (2026-10-18, by design)

Bruce: per-team fuel has to be an empirical measurement of how a team did in that match, so no OPR,

## "Is it shooting" signals in the credit model (2026-10-18, built, not adopted)

**What.** Two per-robot shooting signals, found by the failure finder's launch research
(`data/exp_launch/RESULTS.md`):
* **DINO "is shooting":** a ridge model on the robot crop's DINOv2 embedding, centred per robot and view

## Dense volleys: splitting chained flight tracks (2026-10-18, adopted with a refit credit model)

**Problem.** Bruce saw that 254's volleys barely register in the main camera. In 2026cc_qm24 at 70-74 s (40
scouted balls), the main view shows 254's balls clearly arcing to the blue hub, yet none of the ~117
main-view tracks near 254 was a shot. 50 were "slow", 34 "bad fit" and 33 "not rising". In a dense stream
the greedy flight tracker hooks each ball onto the one before it. A track becomes a 2-4 s zigzag over many
balls (up to 116 points), so its starting velocity is an average over several balls, and it fails the
projectile fit.

**Tried** (switches in `autoscout/pipeline.py`, both off):

* `SPLIT_TRACKS`: cut stored tracks where they hop to another ball (`fuel.split_at_jumps`) before the shot

  of them, because the first sighting of a split piece is 40-150 px above 254's 60 px box.

  top, not on its first sighting.

## Stream separation: ballistic flight tracker (tried 2026-10-18, not adopted)

Volleys are the largest per-video error class: credited launch arcs per scouted ball fall about 5x from small
bursts to 20+ ball volleys (failure-finder cycle 17). In a stream the greedy flight tracker loses most arcs: a
new one-point track predicts zero velocity and grabs the *following* ball, and its wide fixed gate lets tracks
hop between balls. `fuel.BallisticTracker` (opt-in: `Options.flight_tracker = "ballistic"`) uses speed-scaled
gates, one optimal assignment per frame and a two-frame look-ahead to seed new tracks. On a synthetic 20-30
balls/s stream it gets 12/12 smooth arcs vs 0/12 for the greedy tracker.

* 81 matches for the burst counts: the same set without 2026cc_qm14, whose blob log used a wrong match window.
* 103 fully scouted alliances in 53 matches for the scouting comparisons.

## Tracking every ball: flight-tracker recall and 60 Hz (2026-10-18/19, 60 Hz adopted for the re-scan)

**Question.** Bruce, watching the inspector: the video "should be able to track ALL of the balls". How many of
the balls a robot scores become their own launch arc, tied to that robot? Can a better flight tracker, or
analysing every frame (60 Hz) instead of every other one (30 Hz), get close to all of them? And does that make
per-team fuel more accurate?

### How recall is measured (`data/exp_track/tools/track_lab.py`)

* **Truth.** Only moments where exactly one robot of an alliance is scoring count.
  * The alliance's scoreboard rises in an episode, with gaps of at most 2 s between increases.
  * Exactly one of its three robots, all scouted with usable timestamps, has scouted shots then.
  * The other two have none within ±7.5 s.
  * The episode's scoreboard increase is then that robot's balls.
  * Kept only when the scoreboard and the scouts roughly agree (ratio 0.5-2), so the "idle" teammates really
    were idle: 95 episodes, 2,304 balls, 37 teams in 64 scouted Chezy quals.
* **A ball counts for the shooter** if a launch arc meets all three tests:
  * it passes the pipeline's shot test;
  * it heads for the alliance's hub;
  * traced back along its fitted parabola, it rises out of the top of the shooter's box. The arc is tied to
    one robot only: the one whose box top it crossed nearest the centre.
* **Each physical ball counts once.** Arcs with the same traced launch merge: fragments of one ball, and the
  main and end camera seeing the same ball.
* **Recall** is unique balls per scored ball. It can exceed 1 because misses are launched but not scored.
* **False** is the same count credited to the two idle teammates, per scored ball. This is the precision side:
  more arcs only help if they land on the right robot.

### In plain words

The video analysis used to look at every other frame. When a robot fires 20 balls a second, each ball moves about
as far between those frames as the gap to the next ball, so the tracker could not tell the balls apart. It
followed only about one ball in five, and in volleys even fewer.

Two changes fix that:

* Cutting tracks where they jump from one ball to another, the default since 2026-10-18 evening.
* Looking at every frame (60 Hz). On the moments where only one robot was scoring, this finds an arc for about
  every ball the scoreboard counted, in the end cameras, in the main camera and in big volleys.

Finding the balls is only half of the job: each arc also has to be tied to the robot that shot it. About a third
of the extra arcs went to a teammate standing nearby. Once the process-of-elimination linking was in, the extra
arcs paid off: per-team fuel moved about 1.4 balls closer to the scouts' counts (95 % CI 0.2-2.7), and the top
scorer was right more often.

60 Hz costs about twice the CPU of the fuel scan. Limiting it to the seconds before scoreboard jumps does not help,
because at Chezy balls are scored almost all the time.

* **Wiring:** `pipeline.FUEL_60HZ` (off until the full re-scan is in). With it on, `pipeline.load_fuel60` swaps in
  the 60 Hz tracks wherever a scan is loaded for analysis (`reanalyse`, so finalize, and `link_x_ab.py`). The
  tracks are rebuilt by `autoscout/tracks60.py` from `config.BLOBS60` logs with the greedy tracker, exactly as
  the winning arm did. A match without a log keeps its 30 Hz tracks, and `result.json` records `fuel_hz`.
  * The credit model refit on that arm is `data/exp_attrmodel/attr_model_elim60.json` (evidence only, λ 3e-5).
  * `fuel.BallisticTracker` scales itself to 60 Hz too, but it did not beat greedy.
* **Logs:** 60 Hz blob logs (gzip, about 21 MB a match) are in `data/exp_track/blobs60/`.
* **Tools:** `data/exp_track/tools/` (`track_lab.py`, `track_ab.py`, `rescan60.py`, `render_arcs.py`).

## Launches from outside the robot's own zone are not shots (2026-10-18, adopted)

**Rule (Bruce).** In teleop a robot may only score from its own alliance zone (G407). A ball launched while
its robot is in the neutral zone or the opponents' half is a pass or a feed, so it should not count as launch
evidence at all.

**Before.** Zone was a property of the robot, not of the launch. Each scoreboard jump's weights multiplied a
robot's evidence by its zone fraction over the 4.7 s window. The refit credit model trusted that only weakly
(zone power 0.27), because positions near the zone line are noisy. Launches from outside the zone still counted.

**Now** (`pipeline.LAUNCH_ZONE_WEIGHT = 0`, `LAUNCH_ZONE_MARGIN = 1.0`): in teleop, a launch whose robot centre is
more than 1.0 m beyond its own zone line at the launch time is dropped. `result.json` counts them as
`shots_out_of_zone`.

* **How many:** 20.6 % of teleop launches at Chezy (split tracks) start more than 0.6 m beyond the robot's zone
  line, and almost all of those more than 1.5 m. The 1.0 m and 0.6 m margins give the same results.
