  • The main error left: in bursts where one robot shoots, about a third of the arcs get credited to a teammate standing next to it. Picking the right robot for each arc is the next piece of work.
  • Some shooters are never seen by any camera, for example a robot parked right under an end camera. They still get an estimated share from the robots that are not accounted for.
  • Balls are tracked at 60 frames a second where the webcast really has 60 pictures a second (every Chezy match and some Championship ones). Most FIRST webcasts repeat each picture of a 30 fps stream, so those stay at 30.

How a ball gets credited

Read the scoreboard

The broadcast's live fuel counter gives each alliance's total and the moment every ball counted.

Find every robot

A detector boxes robots in all three camera views. Bumper numbers and appearance tie each track to a team.

Follow every ball

Yellow fuel is tracked in the air. Arcs that rise out of a robot are counted as its launches.

Split each jump

When the counter goes up, those balls go to the robots that launched in the seconds before, using only that match's video.

Numbers are from 131 matches across five 2026 events. Per-robot credit uses only that match's video: no OPR, no team history, nothing from later matches, so it works live at a new event.
